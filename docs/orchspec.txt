Medicaid work requirement compliance for re-enrollment is a design problem with law, privacy, multiple agencies, and fragile operational realities. Proposed is a state-level Medicaid Re-enrollment Orchestrator that automatically discovers authoritative data across state/federal systems to confirm activity/exemption status under the HB1 work requirements
a high-level architecture (components and dataflows)


which FHIR resources (beyond meta.security/security labels) to use and how they map to the law’s checks


API/interop patterns (façade, gateways, search/query examples)


identity and privacy-preserving matching options (PPRL, MPI)


consent, Part 2 and other privacy controls (DS4P, Consent, Audit)


operational considerations, fallback, errors, and trust rules


Required are FHIR façade endpoints for each department (Labor, SSA/SSDI proxy, Corrections, Education, Child Welfare/TANF, SNAP, Veterans Affairs, State Health Information Exchange, state immunization/claims stores, etc.). If a department cannot expose FHIR, the façade will translate from CSV/SQL/REST into FHIR.




1 — High level architecture (components & flows)
Front end / Portal


Member fills re-enrollment, optionally provides minimal proof (uploads, attestations).


Portal creates a QuestionnaireResponse/Task and Patient (or reuses existing) and starts orchestration.


Medicaid Re-enrollment Orchestrator (central service)


Orchestrates verification flows, enforces business rules, aggregates results.


Implements eligibility rules (lookback windows, 1–3 months, 80 hours, income thresholds, exemption lists).


Stores decision provenance and reasons as Provenance, AuditEvent, and Task status.


Identity & Matching Service (MPI / PPRL)


Performs deterministic matching (Medicaid ID, SSN if allowed) and probabilistic matching (name, DOB, address) or PPRL (privacy-preserving).


Returns confidence scores and links to authoritative records.


Consent & Authorization Service


Maintains Consent resources (captures the member’s permission to query external agencies).


Issues short-lived authorizations/tokens for façade calls.


Supports emergency/legally required exceptions and revocations.


Departmental FHIR Façades / Gateways (one per partner)


Translates each department’s native API/data into FHIR resources.


Exposes a minimal, authenticated FHIR surface (Patient, Observation, Condition, Coverage, DocumentReference, Claim/ClaimResponse, Endpoint, CoverageEligibilityResponse, etc.) for the orchestrator to query.


Applies coarse access control and logs queries.


Trusted Sources & Cache / Data Lake


Periodic pulls (or subscription/Bulk Data export) to maintain local caches of stable authoritative data (e.g., SSA disability determinations, unemployment wage records) with TTLs.


Cache reduces latency and risk of external outages; each cached item has Provenance and freshness metadata.


Decision Engine & Audit Trail


Evaluates whether the person meets community engagement or exemption criteria.


Produces CoverageEligibilityResponse (or internal decision record) and Provenance/AuditEvent.


Notification & Appeals Workflow


Uses Communication, Task, DocumentReference for notices (mail + electronic) required by law (land mail + one other channel).


Stores notices as DocumentReference with status and provenance.



2 — Key FHIR resources (beyond securityLabel) and how to use them
Below are the most relevant FHIR R4 resources for this use case and how to use them in rule checks.
Patient


Core subject. Link to identifiers (Medicaid ID, MBI, SSA number if allowed) via identifier.


Questionnaire / QuestionnaireResponse


Applicant self-attestation: employment, hours, caregiving status, pregnancy, recent incarceration, education enrollment, program participation. Useful to capture evidence the person provides.


Observation


Represent structured facts from external systems: income amounts, hours worked in a month, unemployment rates for a county, drug treatment enrollment flag, disability determination flags. Use LOINC or custom codes. Example: Observation.code = employment-hours with valueQuantity = 80 and period of month.


Condition


Medical conditions (SUD, pregnancy) flagged as Part 2 sensitive if applicable. For SUD specifics, mark with DS4P labels. Use clinicalStatus and verificationStatus.


Coverage & CoverageEligibilityRequest / CoverageEligibilityResponse


Manage Medicaid enrollment status and eligibility queries. CoverageEligibilityRequest could be used to request a determination; CoverageEligibilityResponse to capture outcome.


Provenance


Record where each piece of evidence came from (agency, timestamp), critical for the “reliable information available to the state” rule and for appeals.


AuditEvent


Record every read/query/access to SUD or other sensitive info for compliance and redisclosure tracking.


Consent


Capture member consent to query and share data across departments. Use fine-grained provisions to limit scope (e.g., allow queries for "Medicaid re-enrollment verification only"). Store revocation timestamps.


DocumentReference


For documents: pay stubs, unemployment letters, SSA letters, court orders, discharge paperwork. Attach PDFs and reference security labels.


Task


Model workflow steps (verify employer claim, fetch SSA disability, follow up for incomplete data). Use task owner, status, and reason.


Bundle


Package multi-source evidence for the applicant’s record or for audit.


List / Group


Group resources for a month’s worth of “activity” evidence.


Practitioner / Organization


Identify originators (e.g., employer attestations, corrections facility).


Endpoint


Where to send queries or receive push updates (subscriptions).


Claim / ClaimResponse


If state chooses to deduce predicted work hours from claims (e.g., billing indicating multiple employer encounters).


ResearchStudy / ResearchSubject (if using deidentified data for program evaluation) — must meet legal constraints.


Subscription / Bulk Data (NDJSON)


For bulk refresh of partner data (monthly/weekly).



3 — Mapping law checks to FHIR resources & rules (examples)
You need to codify each eligibility rule into queries and acceptance logic. examples:
Worked ≥ 80 hours in lookback month


Query employer wage data via Labor façade → returns Observation(employment-hours) or DocumentReference(payroll report).


Or derive hours from Claim/Encounter data if allowed (less reliable).


Rule engine: accept if Observation.value >= 80 and Provenance.source ∈ trusted labor agencies.


Income threshold (monthly or 6-month average)


Query IRS-state wage exchange or DOL / unemployment wage dataset → Observation(monthly-income) with period.


Average across six Observation instances to compute threshold.


Enrolled half-time in education


Use Education system façade: returns Condition(student-status) or Observation(enrollment-status) or DocumentReference(enrollment letter).


Participation in treatment program (exemption)


Query behavioral health / substance treatment registry via SUD provider façade → DocumentReference or Condition flagged with DS4P labels. Only accessible if Consent allows or legal exception applies.


SSDI / Medicare entitlement / veteran disability


Query SSA or Medicaid database Coverage/Condition indications; cache Provenance.


Recent incarceration (in past 3 months)


Corrections façade → Condition/Observation or DocumentReference with incarceration period.


County unemployment rate triggers


Orchestrator computes county unemployment from BLS dataset (bulk import) — record as Observation (county-unemployment-rate).


Short-term hardship (hospitalization, nursing facility)


Query Hospital Discharge / HIE: Encounter with hospitalization codes or Condition flags; accept if Encounter.period overlaps lookback month.



4 — Façade API patterns & example FHIR queries
Façade endpoints should be minimal, authenticated, and return FHIR R4 resources. Examples:
Get monthly employment hours for patient:
 GET /Observation?code=employment-hours&subject=Patient/{id}&date=ge2026-03-01&date=le2026-03-31&_security=urn:gov:confidentiality:42CFRPart2
 — Returns Observation with valueQuantity hours and provenance.


Check SSDI entitlement:
 GET /Condition?code=ssdi-entitlement&subject=Patient/{id}


Fetch recent incarceration:
 GET /DocumentReference?type=corrections-release&subject=Patient/{id}


Bulk refresh SSA disability flags monthly: use Bulk Data Export from SSA façade and import as Observation with Provenance.


Façade design notes:
Use Prefer: return=representation for POSTs.


Accept patient.identifier or hashed identifiers for privacy.


Return standard status codes and explain errors with OperationOutcome.



5 — Identity & matching — strong, practical options
Identity matching is the most delicate part. Consider a layered approach:
Deterministic (first pass)


Match on Medicaid ID (primary). If user presents SSN and law allows usage, use hashed SSN as additional identifier.


Probabilistic / Fuzzy


Name, DOB, address, phone. Return confidence score.


Privacy Preserving Record Linkage (PPRL)


Bloom filters or secure multi-party computation so agencies can match without sharing raw PII. Use hashed tokens or an HL7 FHIR Patient.link with masked identifiers.


Human-in-the-loop


For low confidence matches, open a Task for manual review. Notify applicant to provide an attestation or documents.


Store match metadata in Patient.identifier with use=historic or as Provenance linking to the matched external resource.

6 — Consent, Part 2 / DS4P, and other privacy safeguards
Consent resource: every cross-agency query should be authorized by a Consent resource unless a statutory exception applies (e.g., mandatory reporting, audit, court order). Consent should:


Be purpose-limited to “Medicaid re-enrollment verification”.


Include provision with actor references (which agencies may read) and data references (which resource types are allowed).


Include period (validity window).


DS4P / security labels: mark SUD, mental health, HIV, etc. with meta.security codes (e.g., 42CFRPart2) and use DS4P semantics for redisclosure notices. Enforcement steps:


The Orchestrator and façades filter out SUD data unless Consent permits.


When SUD data is used to make a decision, store a minimal derived flag (e.g., exempt=true) but redact details, and attach Provenance that explains the source and consent. This reduces redisclosure.


Audit and provenance: every read/write triggers AuditEvent; each evidence item gets Provenance with agent = issuing agency. Keep audit logs immutable and searchable for appeals.


Least privilege & data minimization: only request the minimal data required to confirm eligibility (e.g., “hours worked in month” rather than full paystubs), and retain only what law requires.


Part 2 special handling: if SUD treatment records are needed to confirm exemption, require explicit consent and flag redisclosure limits. If a SUD record is returned as proof, the Orchestrator should record only necessary conclusions (e.g., “receives treatment from qualifying program — exempt”) and store the full SUD document only if legally necessary and with strong protections.



7 — Trust rules: what counts as “reliable information available to the state”
The statute allows states to rely on “reliable information available to the state.” Define and publish a Trusted Source Registry listing agency sources and the evidence types you will accept automatically. For example:
SSA — disability entitlement docs (trusted)


State Labor / Unemployment Wage System — monthly wage reports (trusted)


Corrections — incarceration dates (trusted)


Education system — enrollment status (trusted)


State Behavioral Health Registry / Medicaid claims — treatment participation (trusted with consent or legal exception)


SNAP/TANF MIS — participation flags (trusted)


Each registry entry must list: source, resource type, freshness window, minimum provenance, and acceptance logic (e.g., “accept employment hours if source=State DOL and Provenance.signature present”).

8 — Decision engine & example flow
Sequence to check re-enrollment automatically:
Portal receives re-enrollment request (or periodic re-enrollment trigger for active members). Create Task and QuestionnaireResponse.


Orchestrator checks Patient and Consent. If no consent, send Communication to member requesting consent (paper + second channel) — but statute allows state to use reliable info without member submission; the policy must state whether the state will proceed without consent in those cases.


Identity service resolves member. If low confidence, create Task for manual verification.


The orchestrator queries the Trusted Source Registry following acceptance rules (façade calls) for the lookback month(s).


Collect resources (Observations, DocumentReference). Each has Provenance. Evaluate rule sets:


did any Observation.code=employment-hours value >= 80 for any 1 month in lookback? OR income criteria? OR other exemption flags?


Produce decision:


If OK: generate CoverageEligibilityResponse/internal decision and Provenance. Notify member via Communication and mail.


If not OK: generate Task and Communication with notice of noncompliance and explain next steps (30 days to appeal or provide docs). Store notice as DocumentReference.


All access logged in AuditEvent. Appeals tracked via Task.



9 — Error handling, fallback & human review
Missing authoritative data: platform should fall back to asking member to upload evidence (DocumentReference) and create an expedited Task for human review.


Conflicting evidence: apply trust hierarchy (authoritative > derived > self-attestation). If conflict persists, human review required.


Agency outages: use cached data with timestamps and mark freshness in Provenance. If no reliable data and no member documents, send noncompliance notice but retain eligibility until appeal — follow statutory safe harbor.



10 — Auditability, transparency & appeals (statutory requirements)
Keep a complete, queryable ledger of each decision: inputs, which agency provided them, transformation logic, and the rule (e.g., “Met 80 hours via DOL Observation dated 2026-03-15”). Use Provenance + Bundle for the decision package.


Store notices and proof of delivery (mail sent, SMS delivered response) as Communication + DocumentReference.


Support API for third-party advocates/legal reps (with Consent) and generate human-readable decision summaries.



11 — Implementation & governance recommendations
Start with a limited pilot (geography or subset of exemptions) to test identity linkage, façade capabilities, and consent UX.


Create a cross-agency Trusted Source Agreement and MOU for data exchange, defining access controls and PII handling. Use Contract resource to represent these agreements.


Publish the acceptance rules (so clinics & members know what evidence the state will accept automatically).


Design for privacy by default: minimal data pulled; only store conclusions for cases where details are not necessary.


Leverage standards & IGs: DS4P for privacy labels; US Core for clinical data; IG for Consent workflows; Bulk FHIR for periodic refresh.


Logging & retention: keep audit logs per regulatory minimums and make them immutable.


Accessibility & user notification: the statute requires mail + one other: implement both and log delivery.

13 — Practical constraints & legal/privacy caveats
Part 2 / SUD records: must be handled with DS4P labels and explicit consent for queries/disclosure; preferred design is to infer exemption (yes/no) from SUD registry without storing detailed SUD clinical data.


SSN usage: state law often restricts use/storage of SSNs; prefer hashed tokens or MPI with secure vaulting.


Inter-agency legal agreements: required for many exchanges (QSOAs, MOUs).


Time constraints in law: June 1, 2026 interim rules — you must plan sprinted piloting now to meet operational readiness.



14 — Quick roadmap (practical phased rollout)
Phase 0 — governance & pilot agreements: Trusted Source Registry, MOUs, policy for PII and Part 2.


Phase 1 — pilot orchestrator & core façades: Labor, SSA flags, Corrections, Medicaid claims; build identity service & Consent engine.


Phase 2 — expand connectors: Education, SNAP/TANF, VA, inpatient hospital data; add cache and Bulk Data ingestion.


Phase 3 — scale & audit: Production rollouts, public documentation of acceptance rules, monitoring & appeals workflows.
